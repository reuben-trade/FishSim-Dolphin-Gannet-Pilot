GANNET DEPARTURE AND BENEFIT THROUGH TIME — 9 NOVEMBER 2026

Scope: the corrected 60-run, 36T H × G discovery bank. There are 36 mixed
runs, 12 gannet-only controls and 12 dolphin-only controls, reusing four
school geometries. All source configurations and consumed files were sealed;
no behaviour, probability, movement rule or native simulation was changed.

HOW COMMON IS THE DEFAULT'S BEHAVIOUR?
The strict observed pattern is: every gannet ends in search, no bird has an
accessible new target, and fish are shallow enough but beyond the travel gate.
This occurs in only 2/48 bird-bearing runs: 1/36 mixed and 1/12 gannet-only.

School      Treatment                 Last launch  Last water entry  Last bout end
39061001    H1 / 6G / mixed (default)  17.6675T     19.4128T          19.5638T
39061002    6G / gannets only          14.1280T     13.7243T          16.0513T

All birds enter search by 21.8267T and 18.8057T respectively. Their sampled
whole-school depth blackouts are 17.3–31.9T and 16.4–26.9T. Shallow fish later
return, but straight-flight search has taken the birds beyond the travel gate.
The second case contains no dolphins, so upward dolphin herding cannot be the
sole explanation or solution for this search/reacquisition limitation.

Four runs have no new launch during the final 6T. The other two are school
39061001 G-only/1G (last launch 29.0637T) and school 39061002 H3/3G mixed
(29.7137T). They end orienting/returning with eligible targets, not with the
whole flock lost. A long launch gap must not be called permanent cessation.
Indeed, 20/48 runs launch again after a gap of at least 6T, and 33/48 have a
committed attack in progress at the observation boundary.

At bird level, 14/160 saved bird trajectories end searching without accessible
prey: 12 belong to the two full-flock losses; two more are G0/G2 in school
39063502 H2/3G, where fish are still too deep. These are repeated trajectories,
not 160 independent biological samples.

DOES BENEFIT DROP WHEN ATTACKS CEASE?
The plots compare each mixed species with its own matched solo control:
same school and H for dolphins; same school and bird count for gannets.
They show cumulative percent gain, absolute cumulative captures/predator
advantage, and the trailing 6T capture-rate advantage. A zero control gives
an undefined percentage, not zero or infinity. The first 6T has no complete
trailing window. Raw mixed/control counts accompany the exported data.

The default has a short dolphin-benefit dip: cumulative gain is +20% at
19.6T, reaches 0% around 20–21T, then recovers and ends at +17.39% (27 vs
23 captures). Thus the full curve does not show a persistent collapse after
gannet attacks cease. It should not be summarized as never dipping.

Using equal, fully observed 6T windows around its final committed bout end
(19.56375T), dolphins capture 5 vs 4 in the preceding window and 9 vs 7 in
the following window. Relative gain is +25% before and +28.57% after;
absolute rate advantage changes from +0.01389 to +0.02778 captures/predator/T.
This is one trajectory comparison with integer counts, not a causal test.

Gannets already trail their control before the last launch: 2 vs 5 captures
at 17.7T. Their cumulative gap later widens to 2 vs 7 (-71.43%). A recent
window with zero catches in both runs is not improved gannet feeding, even
though its absolute difference is zero; its percentage is undefined.

Only 2/36 mixed runs have complete 6T windows on both sides of the last
launch; dolphin rate advantage rises in both. Only the default has complete
6T windows around the final committed attack ending. In 24/36 runs a gannet
attack is unfinished at 36T, and 11 end too late for a full post-window.
There is therefore no basis here for a general claim that benefit declines
after cessation, nor for a general claim that cessation has no effect.

HOW TO READ THE MARKERS
In the exported PNG/PDF figures, solid burgundy is the last realised launch;
dashed blue is the last committed water entry. The browser has its own legend.
Dotted grey: end of the final committed plunge/pursuit, if observed. Shading
spans launch to that end. Ascent and prey responses can continue afterward.
An uncompleted bout has no invented ending. T is model time, not seconds.
In cessation_summary.csv, last_gannet_bout_end_T means the latest observed
closed bout. For final attack cessation, use benefit_by_time's final active-end
marker and right-censoring flag; an unfinished later bout is not a cessation.

NEXT MODEL TESTS
An explicit dolphin surface-herding objective and a separate gannet holding/
reacquisition rule would address different mechanisms. The observed default
does not justify assuming flock departure is common across this bank. Neither
behaviour has been changed here; fish depth and contact probability are not
adjusted to manufacture continued feeding.
