fig1_model: The shared dolphin cycle and an illustrative encounter. Dolphins maintain near-prey pressure for H, select feasible prey with few neighbours, strike once, then recover. Top and side views show school 39061002 at 12T, with paths and captures over 6–12T. Horizontal coordinates are relative to the live-fish centroid; z=0 is the surface. The historical frame is illustrative, not evidence of completed herding or causal mediation.

fig2_returns: Absolute returns (a,b) and matched partner effects (c,d) over complete 36T encounters, with equal weights for four previously observed schools. Solid lines join means at actual numeric bird counts. Dashed baselines are same-H dolphin-only returns (a) and count-matched gannet-only returns (b). Partner effects compare mixed with these controls; faint points are the four paired school effects and ribbons/whiskers are the existing 95% pointwise whole-school bootstrap intervals. Points have slight horizontal jitter. Absolute food per bird declines generally as counts grow, while some adjacent counts rise; partner benefits can remain positive. The intervals are exploratory and do not correct selection or multiplicity.

fig3_opportunities: Descriptive opportunity and prey-state diagnostics at every dolphin-bearing setting. Faint points show all four schools. Lines join equal-school means except in (b), where the line is pooled eligible contacts divided by pooled launches; faint points remain individual school ratios. Launches and contacts include the full 36T encounter; a launch near its end can remain incomplete. The saved r90 measure is the 90th percentile of distances of living fish from their current three-dimensional centroid, trapezoid-time-averaged over observed nonempty frames. Consumption and survivor selection affect this measure. The plots describe associations, not a causal separation of herding, disturbance and competition.

fig4_sensitivity: Raw school effects and numerical sensitivity, with D denoting dolphins and G gannets. (a,b) H1/nine-bird and H2/twelve-bird discovery settings: colours connect the same four schools, diamonds are means, and whiskers are saved 95% pointwise whole-school bootstrap intervals. Dolphin effects use same-H D-only controls; gannet effects use count-matched G-only controls. (c,d) A distinct eight-school H=2T, three-bird comparison, with schools connected across dt=0.00125T and dt=0.000625T. Diamonds are means; only coarse primary means have the saved 97.5% marginal intervals, chosen for the two-species primary comparison. All axes show captures per individual predator over 36T (12 dolphins; nine/twelve birds above and three below). These eight schools do not validate the selected discovery settings; repeating a school on a numerical grid does not add a school.
